## Writing for readers

Write documentation, UI text, captions, and reports for their intended reader.
Authoring instructions should shape the result without becoming its subject.

- Describe what the artifact does and what the reader needs to use or understand it.
- Mention exclusions, limitations, or design choices only when they affect a
  reader's decision or prevent a plausible misunderstanding.
- Avoid generic praise and obvious labels such as “reusable operations.” Use
  concrete headings such as “Sampling features.”
- Keep implementation and development-process details in developer notes unless
  they change the public behavior or usage.
- After a correction, revise the passage as a whole. Remove echoes of the prompt,
  repeated assurances, and discussion of alternatives the reader was never offered.
