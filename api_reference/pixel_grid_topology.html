Pixel-grid topology and gradients#

The mesh builder creates a triangle mesh from a regular grid of cells centered on source-image pixels. Each live cell contributes a quad whose initial corners are at pixel corners. Neighbor affinities determine which corner vertices are shared.

Training minimizes the expected rendered loss of sampled meshes. At inference, construct one deterministic mesh from the predicted decisions.

For function signatures, see the module reference. The optimization tutorial shows how to use these pieces together.

Inputs and outputs#

v, vt0, vt1, vi, vertex_opacity, face_cell_ids, face_ids = pixel_grid_mesh(
    depth_z, displacement_xyz, intrinsics,
    alive, join_right, join_down,
    diagonal=None,
    feather_width=None,
    return_metadata=False,
)

The function returns seven elements. Optional outputs are None when disabled.

Boolean connection to the right neighbor

feather_width

N × H × W or None

Nonnegative strip width in source-image pixels

Each of the N meshes has its own topology, corner fans, and feather boundaries. A camera supplied as 1 × 3 × 3 is shared across the batch. Shared floating inputs supplied with expand receive the sum of their sample gradients. Floating inputs share float32/float64 dtype and device; hard decisions are bool. Depth, K, displacement, and widths must be finite and geometrically valid. The displaced surface must remain in front of the source camera.

Output

Meaning

v

Final source-camera 3D positions, already fitted and displaced, including strips when enabled

vt0

vt1

Source-camera projection of the final positions; may leave [0,1]

vi

Compact int32 triangle indices

vertex_opacity

Generated material-opacity multiplier: core 1, outer strip 0; None without strips

face_cell_ids

Source-cell ownership, or None unless metadata is requested

face_ids

Stable cell/local-face identifiers, or None unless metadata is requested

Vertices have shape N × Vmax × 3 and faces N × Fmax × 3, where the maximum counts are taken across the batch. Each sample occupies a compact prefix; indices and stable IDs are local to that sample. Padding has face indices and metadata -1, positions (0,0,1), and zero UVs and opacity. It contributes no gradients. vi[..., 0] >= 0 marks valid faces. Empty meshes and empty batches are supported. The same seven tuple entries are returned, including None for disabled outputs.

Faces are emitted in increasing stable-ID order, including strips. Removing unrelated cells preserves relative tie order for the renderer’s equal-depth triangles. The reference function uses the same order.

vertex_opacity defines the boundary fade. Pass it as opacity=vertex_opacity to the transparent renderer to multiply the appearance alpha. None means an all-one multiplier.

Project v into the desired target camera before rendering: the transparent renderer accepts target pixel XY and camera Z. Both UVs use 2D interpolation. Use source-feature sampling to account for occlusion when transferring features to the deformed mesh.

Pixel and camera convention#

Source pixel (x,y) has its center at integer coordinates. Its corners are (x±0.5,y±0.5). Normalized texture UV is ((x+0.5)/W,(y+0.5)/H), without a vertical flip. A K resized by sx,sy needs fx’=sx·fx and cx’=sx·(cx+0.5)−0.5, and likewise for Y. The same convention must be used when resizing the source image and its camera.

Depth is camera Z; displacement is expressed in the same camera frame. Apply world and placement transforms after building the mesh.

Cuts and corner sharing#

Connection decisions are undirected and exist once per neighboring pair. A connection requires both cells alive. Alive/dead borders and the outer grid boundary are exposed boundaries, regardless of a join bit.

An interior cut consisting of one isolated grid edge is ignored. It survives when either endpoint touches another cut edge, or an already exposed boundary. “Border” includes the outside grid and a live/dead boundary, including holes. Two remote isolated cuts do not support each other. Two cuts meeting at a corner do. Classification uses the original sampled cuts simultaneously, not an iterative endpoint-pruning algorithm that would erase a two-edge chain.

Construct a connected fan among the at-most-four live cell corners incident on each lattice corner. Give each fan one shared vertex. Diagonally touching but unconnected cells remain separate. Connections elsewhere in the mesh never weld a local cut. An open fan can contain all four incident cells: its two boundary edges share one crack-tip vertex. Interior cuts with both tips on one edge are removed by the isolated-cut rule.

Cut realization depends only on sampled bits. The effective probability that an edge is cut is therefore not simply its raw Bernoulli probability. Training differentiates the distribution of proposals followed by this exact deterministic realization.

Closed-form displaced corners and diagonals#

For a cell c and its source corner q, form the proposal

\[Y_{cq}=d_c K^{-1}[q_x,q_y,1]^T+\Delta_c.\]

For a corner fan S, use its least-squares fit

\[V_{q,S}=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{c\in S}Y_{cq}.\]

The average is confined to each fan, including its shared crack tip. Gradients propagate through depth, displacement, and K. Averaging the shared corners can shift a cell center away from its individual displacement proposal.

Each live quad emits two triangles. Automatic diagonal choice compares the two diagonal midpoints to the requested displaced cell center in 3D, choosing the smaller squared error and TL–BR on a tie. The decision is discrete. Pass sampled diagonal bits to learn it from rendered loss. Regularize the predicted geometry to avoid degeneracies and folds.

Optional boundary feathering#

Feathering extrudes source-screen-normal strips at the fitted boundary depth. Widths at a boundary vertex average the two incident boundary-cell widths in that fan. Separate sides of a cut do not share widths except at a shared tip. The construction uses shared miter joins where bounded, bevels otherwise, and caps for reversals. All outer vertices carry opacity multiplier zero; the core vertices remain one. Linear interpolation gives the fade across each strip.

Original UVs extend constantly across the strip; reprojected UVs use the new positions. The network supplies only width. Geometry and width receive ordinary render gradients. The zero-alpha outer contour contributes no artificial coverage jump to the existing edge estimator.

Large widths, acute crack tips, nearby opposing boundaries, and folded geometry can cause the extruded strips to overlap and accumulate opacity. Keep strips narrow. Switching between miter and bevel joins is discrete.

Stochastic topology objective#

Let independent proposal bits have p=σ(η), and let M include the cut cleanup. The intended objective is

\[F(\eta,x)=\mathbb E_z[\mathcal L(R(M(z,x)))].\]

For one bit,

\[\frac{\partial F}{\partial\eta_i} =p_i(1-p_i)\mathbb E_{z_{-i}}[\mathcal L(z_i=1)-\mathcal L(z_i=0)].\]

bernoulli_render_loss uses ARM with the full scalar loss. For shared uniform u, sample z⁺=1[u>1−p] and z⁻=1[u<p]. Its logit estimate is

\[g_\eta=(\mathcal L(z^+)-\mathcal L(z^-))(u-\tfrac12).\]
loss = bernoulli_render_loss(logits, evaluate, pairs=1, generator=rng)
loss.backward()

The callback receives hard decisions with shape [2 * pairs, *logits.shape], builds and renders the meshes, and returns a [2 * pairs] vector of losses. The first half contains z⁺ and the second half the matching z⁻. Each loss is evaluated independently of the other sampled topologies. The helper averages the losses and supplies the ARM gradient to logits while preserving ordinary continuous gradients from each evaluation. If the callback has other randomness, keep it fixed within a pair or independent of topology sampling. Define target validity and loss normalization independently of predicted alive bits, so missing geometry contributes to the loss.

Averaging losses, E[L(I)], and evaluating the loss of an averaged image, L(E[I]), optimize different objectives. The latter can reward a mixture of topologies that cannot be represented by a single mesh.

The estimator remains valid after isolated-cut cleanup because cleanup is a deterministic function of the sampled bits. A cut with no rendering consequence has zero expected loss gradient; nearby views, geometry evidence and initialization matter.

Supported inputs and limitations#

pixel_grid_mesh accepts float32/float64 CUDA tensors. Its reference counterpart also supports CPU tensors. CUDA graph capture is unsupported. First-order gradients propagate from positions, reprojected UVs, and feather geometry to depth, displacement, intrinsics, and feather width. Backward is not guaranteed to be bitwise deterministic.

The ARM estimator can have high variance on large grids. Increasing the pair count trades additional evaluations for lower sampling variance. Geometry regularization, loss design, optimization schedules, and convergence remain the caller’s responsibility. Absent cells must still contribute to the image loss; masking evaluation by predicted visibility can hide missing geometry.
